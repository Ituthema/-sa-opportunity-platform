# Editorial Workflow and Listing Standards

## 1. Governing rule

A human researches, enters, verifies, interprets, approves and publishes every opportunity. Software provides administrative support only. No automated process may make factual or editorial decisions.

## 2. Research and capture

For each candidate opportunity, the editor should manually consult the organisation's official advert, official careers page, official institution page or another primary source appropriate to the opportunity.

Capture the source URL and the facts actually stated. Do not rely on an aggregator as the sole evidence for a listing when an official source can be identified. If an official source cannot be found, keep the candidate unpublished and document the limitation for human review.

Record, where available:

- Opportunity title and category.
- Recruiting or offering organisation.
- Location and province.
- Description and duties or programme details.
- Minimum qualification and NQF level, only if stated.
- Age, experience and other eligibility requirements, only if stated.
- Stipend, salary, allowance or funding amount, only if stated.
- Opening date and closing date, including the source's wording.
- Official application instructions and application URL.
- Source publication date, if stated.
- Date the editor checked the source.
- Editor identity and evidence notes.

Use explicit labels such as "Not stated in the advert" or "Not confirmed" rather than filling gaps with assumptions.

## 3. Verification and editorial approval are different

**Verification** is a human assessment that the captured factual details accurately reflect the identified source at the recorded time.

**Editorial approval** is a separate human decision that the listing is suitable, clear, lawful and useful to publish.

One person may perform both roles in an initial single-editor operation, but the system must record the two decisions distinctly. A future multi-editor workflow may require a second reviewer.

## 4. State transitions

Recommended sequence:

1. Draft — record is being prepared.
2. Awaiting verification — source and facts need checking.
3. Verified — a human has checked the record against its source.
4. Awaiting editorial approval — verified record awaits publication decision.
5. Approved — an authorised human has approved publication.
6. Published — public page is available.
7. Closed — a human has confirmed that the opportunity is no longer open.
8. Archived — retained for historical/reference purposes and not presented as open.
9. Rejected — not suitable for publication; retain an internal reason where appropriate.

A technical validator may block an incomplete record from advancing, but it cannot mark a record Verified, Approved or Published.

## 5. Closing dates and stale listings

A date calculation may flag a record whose closing date has passed. It must not automatically change the record to Closed or Archived. An editor must check the official source or seek appropriate confirmation and record the decision.

If the official source changes a deadline, record the new date, the date checked, the source evidence and the human who made the correction. Preserve a meaningful change history.

If the source disappears or the application link fails, flag the listing for human review. A broken URL alone does not prove that an opportunity is closed or fraudulent.

## 6. Publication requirements

Before publication, require:

- A clear title and category.
- Organisation identity.
- A credible official source URL or a documented reason why the source is primary and appropriate.
- Captured facts that are traceable to the source.
- Human verification event with reviewer and timestamp.
- Separate human editorial approval event.
- Clear application instructions and official application route.
- Honest treatment of missing or ambiguous requirements.
- A visible last-verified date.
- A closing-date status that does not overstate certainty.

Do not publish fabricated examples, placeholder opportunities or unverified listings as real openings.

## 7. Public presentation

Make clear that the platform is an independent information service, not the employer or funder. Applicants should follow the official application channel. Do not request application fees or imply that paying the platform improves selection chances.

Show the source link and last-verified date prominently. Distinguish open, closing soon, closed and archived records. "Closing soon" may be a deterministic date-based display, but the underlying opportunity status remains subject to human confirmation.

## 8. Technical support allowed

The system may enforce required fields, validate date formats, calculate date differences, test URL reachability, flag duplicate candidates, create reminders, generate reports and maintain audit logs. These checks are advisory or structural; they do not prove factual truth.

## 9. Corrections and complaints

Provide a way for visitors to report inaccurate or outdated information without collecting unnecessary personal data. A report creates a human review task; it does not automatically change a listing. Record the investigation, outcome and any correction or status change.
